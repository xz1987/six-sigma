## 2. User and use case
- **Primary user:** The lead EMS worker on the responding ambulance who will arrive at the incident scene, working across five selected Queens neighborhoods. A dispatcher supervisor / analyst is a secondary user of historical analysis.
- **Decision supported:** The responding lead EMS worker monitors their assigned synthetic incident, elapsed response time, and arrival status. The simulated feed automatically supplies the on-scene confirmation; no manual arrival click is required in version one. After completion, the worker and secondary supervisor review incidents and neighborhoods requiring investigation. Completed-response QC flags do not provide routing or clinical instructions.
- **Review workflow:** Run a simulated dispatch feed that automatically delivers synthetic incident updates to a Shiny active-call view and supervisor analysis dashboard. Retain CSV upload for historical baseline records and reproducible demos, with end-of-year summaries of available data. All simulated views carry a visible synthetic-data label.
- **Prompt fit:** Incident-level monitoring exposes variation and extreme delays that monthly averages can hide. A flag requests investigation; it does not establish a cause, clinical outcome, or staff fault.

## 3. Inputs and measurement
One historical CSV row represents one completed dispatched incident. A deterministic replay feed also emits synthetic dispatch and on-scene events. All demonstration data are explicitly labeled synthetic.

| Column | Type / units | Meaning and source |
|---|---|---|
| `incident_id` | unique text | Synthetic incident identifier; an operational equivalent would come from dispatch logs. |
| `zone` | categorical: `Astoria`, `Flushing`, `Jamaica`, `Forest Hills`, `Far Rockaway` | Selected Queens neighborhood containing the incident; not ambulance home zone. |
| `call_priority` | ordered categories: `High`, `Medium`, `Low` | Synthetic urgency ratings: most urgent, urgent, and lower urgency respectively. Required for stratified analysis; not an official EMS classification. |
| `weather_condition` | categorical: `Dry`, `Rain`, `Snow/Ice`, `Unknown` | Synthetic conditions at the incident location at assignment time. Dry means no rain or snow/ice; Snow/Ice takes precedence for mixed winter conditions; Rain otherwise denotes rain; Unknown means unavailable. Synthetic context and, in the separate experimental ETA dataset, a potential predictor; not official weather observations. |
| `dispatch_time` | ISO 8601 timestamp with UTC offset | Time a specific ambulance unit is assigned to the incident; response clock starts. The existing column name is retained for compatibility. |
| `on_scene_time` | ISO 8601 timestamp with UTC offset | Timestamp automatically supplied by the simulated on-scene event, representing crew confirmation that the ambulance has stopped at the incident scene; response clock stops. Not proximity or patient contact. |

- **Experimental weather schema (H-10):** Add `wind_speed_mph` (nonnegative sustained speed), `wind_gust_mph` (nonnegative gust, at least sustained speed when both exist), and `precipitation_intensity` (`None`, `Light`, `Moderate`, `Heavy`, `Unknown`) in a separate versioned ETA fixture. Values describe conditions known at assignment; document wind averaging/gust observation windows in its codebook. Missing or invalid numeric weather becomes NA with an issue count, never zero; missing/invalid intensity becomes Unknown. Otherwise valid incidents remain eligible for existing QC even when ETA inputs are incomplete.
- **Separate hazard descriptions:** Retain raw wind measurements; derive display-only Beaufort categories from sustained wind using the [NWS scale](https://www.weather.gov/boi/beaufort), with exact conversion boundaries and tests documented under H-10/H-11. Do not apply Beaufort to gusts or treat categories as equal increments in travel delay. Precipitation labels are synthetic until a type-specific observational standard and thresholds are documented; they must not be advertised as official ratings. Never combine rain, ice and wind into an invented official severity score or assign an official winter-storm impact index from these fields.
- **Geographic scope:** Five selected Queens neighborhoods, not complete coverage of the borough or official EMS districts. Assign neighborhood labels directly in the synthetic feed; address geocoding and geographic boundary assignment are outside version one. Neighborhood names follow [NYC Queens community-board descriptions](https://www.nyc.gov/site/communityboards/about/queens-boards.page).
- **Analysis groups:** Five neighborhoods times three priorities gives 15 distinct groups. Meeting the 100-incident baseline threshold for every group requires at least 1,500 valid baseline incidents, with each group represented in all 12 baseline months. Priority categories describe simulated urgency only; they do not prescribe clinical triage or service targets.
- **Simulated event contract:** Each event has a unique `event_id`, `incident_id`, `event_type` (`dispatched`, optional `approaching_scene`, or `on_scene`), `event_time` with UTC offset, zone, and priority. `dispatched` represents unit assignment and sets `dispatch_time`; the feed automatically emits confirmed arrival and sets `on_scene_time` from its event timestamp, not the browser receipt time. No separate manual arrival submission is implemented. Optional `approaching_scene` represents entry into a simulated 500-foot radius, changes display status only, and does not stop the response clock. It does not require real GPS or a routing engine. Active incidents may lack arrival time and remain pending, rather than being rejected as invalid completed records. Replayed duplicate events must not create duplicate incidents; invalid or out-of-order events are reported.
- **Simulation controls:** Start, pause/resume, and reset a deterministic replay from documented synthetic fixtures. Display simulated time separately from wall-clock time. Keep session state isolated; restarting restores the documented initial state.
- **Derived measurement:** `response_minutes = (on_scene_time - dispatch_time)` in minutes after converting timestamps to UTC. Label this assignment-to-confirmed-on-scene response time. Apply identical event definitions to baseline and monitoring records. On-scene means the vehicle has stopped at the scene, consistent with [NEMSIS eTimes.06](https://www.nemsis.org/media/nemsis_v3/release-3.5.1/DataDictionary/APIs/EMSDataSetAPI/eTimes_v3_xsd.html); it does not mean patient contact.
- **Validation:** For completed records, report and exclude incidents with missing incident ID, neighborhood, priority, assignment or arrival timestamps; unparseable timestamps; negative durations; duplicate incident IDs; or undocumented neighborhood/priority categories. Weather is explicitly exempt from exclusion: missing or invalid weather maps to `Unknown`, is reported, and does not exclude an otherwise valid incident from baseline counts, QC, or descriptive analysis. Active-call missing arrival remains pending under the event contract. Report counts and reasons; do not impute response times or remove valid long responses.
- **Cross-zone dispatch:** Assign analysis to the incident zone. Ambulance home zone may be an optional descriptive field; it does not change the grouping definition.
- **Analysis settings:** Explicit baseline start/end and monitoring dates; monitoring begins after the baseline ends. Assign baseline/monitoring membership by unit-assignment timestamp. Event definitions are confirmed for the synthetic demo; any future operational integration must verify the source-system meanings.
- **Weather context:** Use `weather_condition` at assignment time in CSV records and dispatch events; retain it through arrival. Missing weather fields or values map to `Unknown` with a reported count, without excluding otherwise valid incidents from QC. Report invalid nonempty labels as weather validation issues and map them to `Unknown`. Show Unknown as its own descriptive category. Weather is synthetic, supplied by fixtures rather than a live weather API.
- **Weather interpretation:** Weather comparisons are descriptive within a selected neighborhood and priority. They do not establish causation or adjust measured response times, baseline limits, or investigation flags. A separate experimental ETA model may use weather predictors after H-10/H-11 validation. Filtering weather changes descriptive views only; I-MR charts and flags retain the approved neighborhood/priority baseline and sequence.
- Traffic, density, speed limits, hospital distance, and GPS coordinates are not required inputs for version one.

## 4. Statistics and validation
- **Core method:** Individuals and moving-range (I-MR) charts for incident response times, separately by incident zone and call priority, in chronological order. Estimate fixed limits from an initial 12-month baseline and apply them to subsequent monitoring observations.
- **Why it fits:** Each incident supplies one continuous measurement. The method monitors unusual response times and changes between consecutive incidents within a comparable group.
- **Reference:** [NIST individuals control charts](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc322.htm).
- **Individuals calculation:** Baseline mean `xbar`; consecutive absolute moving ranges with mean `MRbar`; `sigma_hat = MRbar / 1.128`; individuals limits `xbar +/- 3 * sigma_hat`. Retain mathematical limits; explain that negative response times are invalid even if the calculated lower limit is negative.
- **Completed records only:** Feed arrivals finalize response durations and pass valid completed incidents to the same tested QC functions used by CSV analysis. Active-call elapsed time is descriptive, not a completed response duration or I-MR observation. Feed updates never change the frozen baseline.
- **Primary investigation rule:** A monitoring response time strictly above its group's fixed individuals upper control limit flags that incident and its zone for investigation. Show the time, value, limit, priority, and baseline dates.
- **Variation chart:** Moving-range center `MRbar`, lower limit zero, upper limit `3.267 * MRbar`. Label upper-limit exceedances as variation signals, distinct from unusually long response times. Calculate ranges only within the same zone/priority sequence, ordered by assignment timestamp with incident ID as a deterministic tie-breaker. The first completed monitoring observation uses the last baseline observation in that same group as its predecessor; later completed observations use the preceding observation in assignment order. Never form ranges across groups. The boundary range is a monitoring value and is excluded from baseline MRbar and limit estimation. When arrivals complete out of assignment order, recompute the affected monitoring sequence from available completed records; pending incidents have no completed-duration observation.
- **Supporting descriptive summaries:** Valid incident count, median, and 90th-percentile response time in minutes, using R's `quantile(..., type = 7)`. These require their own known-answer checks before display.
- **Additional descriptive charts:** Neighborhood response-time boxplots for a selected priority and date range; hour-of-day by weekday heatmap of median response minutes for a selected neighborhood/priority; weather-condition boxplots for a selected neighborhood/priority and date range. Include sample counts and units on every chart. These charts use completed valid incidents and do not create additional investigation rules.
- **Chart definitions:** Derive assignment hour (0-23) and weekday in `America/New_York`, accounting for daylight saving time. Heatmap cells show median and count; empty cells show no data, never zero response time. Boxplots use type-7 quartiles, whiskers to the most extreme observations within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the quartiles, and separate markers beyond the whiskers. Markers are descriptive, not I-MR signals or grounds for deletion. Sparse groups display individual points and counts; no precision or adjusted-ranking claim is made.
- **Chart validation:** Under H-03, independently verify grouping, medians, counts, quartiles, whiskers, weather-missing handling, and local-time conversion before rendering under H-04/H-05. Include an empty heatmap cell and a sparse weather category.
- **Zone comparison:** Present groups side by side, with sample counts, without claiming an adjusted ranking or comparing raw flag totals as equivalent evidence. Priority stratification does not adjust for weather, staffing, traffic, or other case-mix differences.
- **Assumptions:** Assess baseline stability, distribution shape, and serial dependence within each group. Use time plots, distribution diagnostics, and lag diagnostics. Strong skew or dependence can invalidate conventional limit interpretation; revisit the method before presenting validated signals if diagnostics are unsuitable. A 12-month baseline spans a full annual cycle but does not automatically adjust for seasonality. Review monthly patterns; unresolved seasonal structure requires revisiting the method before interpreting fixed limits.
- **Baseline eligibility:** Select 12 consecutive calendar months preceding monitoring. Each zone/priority group needs at least 100 valid incidents, with observations in every baseline month. Report monthly counts. The count and coverage thresholds are project safeguards, not proof of stability or a full seasonal adjustment. Groups failing either threshold show `insufficient baseline data`; descriptive summaries remain available, but monitoring flags are disabled.
- **Threshold reference:** [Minitab control-chart guidance](https://support.minitab.com/minitab/media/pdfs/translate/Assistant_Variables_Control_Charts.pdf) supports 100 observations for control-limit precision. Requiring coverage in all 12 months is a team-chosen safeguard.
- **Baseline review:** One teammate prepares chronological plots, preliminary I-MR charts, distribution diagnostics, and lag/autocorrelation diagnostics; another reviews them. Record group, baseline dates, reviewer, decision, and rationale. Review unusual points, shifts, trends, strong skew, serial dependence, and changes in dispatch procedures, priority definitions, or recording practices. See [NIST process-stability guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section4/ppc45.htm).
- **Approval and exceptions:** Before documented approval, or while concerns remain unresolved, show `baseline requires review` and disable monitoring flags. Review may approve the baseline with documented justification or mark it `baseline unsuitable` pending a revised period or method. Do not use a single normality test as automatic approval. Investigate valid extreme observations rather than automatically deleting them; correct documented errors and record any justified exclusions or reference-period changes. Do not repeatedly remove points merely to obtain a passing chart.
- **Degenerate baseline:** A zero average moving range produces `degenerate baseline`; monitoring flags are disabled. Approval cannot override insufficient data or zero variation.
- **Fixed reference:** Once eligible and approved, freeze the baseline observations and limits during monitoring. Any later rebaseline requires an explicit, documented review; monitoring observations do not silently update limits.
- **Known-answer check:** For the arithmetic fixture `4, 5, 6, 5, 4` minutes: mean `4.8`, moving ranges `1, 1, 1, 1`, `MRbar = 1`, `sigma_hat = 1 / 1.128`, individuals UCL approximately `7.459574`, LCL approximately `2.140426`, and MR UCL `3.267`. A subsequent value `8` exceeds the individuals UCL. The baseline-to-monitoring range is `abs(8 - 4) = 4`, which also exceeds the MR UCL `3.267`; neither baseline estimate changes. This five-point fixture tests arithmetic only, not baseline adequacy.
- **Degenerate checks:** Constant baseline, one observation, missing or invalid timestamps, empty groups, duplicate IDs, tied timestamps, and unequal group sizes. A zero moving range must produce an explicit degenerate-baseline status rather than divide-by-zero behavior or a validated all-clear.
- **Implementation:** Statistical calculations live in plain, framework-independent functions in `app/qc.R`; the UI calls them. Use `testthat` for known-answer and edge-case checks. Verify each reported statistic independently before styling.
- **Repository dependency:** Statistical implementation follows `.claude/skills/stats-first-steering/SKILL.md`, restored by the repository merge.

### Experimental weather-informed arrival estimate (H-10 through H-12)
- **Purpose:** Give the responding lead EMS worker an estimated assignment-to-on-scene duration and arrival-time range at assignment, labeled `Experimental — synthetic data`. This is a broad incident-duration prediction, not GPS routing, patient-contact prediction, or validated operational guidance.
- **Candidate model:** Start with an interpretable R regression of log positive response minutes on neighborhood, priority, assignment-time hour/weekday, weather type, precipitation intensity, and numerical sustained wind/gust features. Specify the final formula, category encoding, collinearity handling and prediction method in H-11 before implementation. Severity categories do not translate directly into minutes. Keep ETA functions in `app/eta.R`, separate from `app/qc.R`.
- **Timing:** Use only inputs available at assignment, never actual arrival or eventual response duration. Add predicted duration to assignment time to obtain the arrival estimate. Predictions are fixed at assignment; do not show a continually recalculated remaining-time estimate without a separately validated conditional model. If simulated time passes the predicted window, show that it has elapsed rather than a negative countdown.
- **Uncertainty:** Show a 95% prediction interval for an individual future duration and the corresponding arrival timestamps. If using log-scale regression, exponentiating the fitted log response gives a conditional median, not the arithmetic mean; label it appropriately. Validate prediction intervals and their assumptions, not only coefficient estimates. See [NIST prediction intervals](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section5/pmd512.htm).
- **Validation:** Use chronological training/validation/test partitions and report their dates. Freeze model choices before the untouched final test; keep shared weather-event blocks together to prevent leakage. Compare with an otherwise comparable model without weather, reporting held-out MAE in minutes, 95% interval coverage and widths, sample counts, and performance by priority/weather category. Set quantitative acceptance gates before fitting in H-11. Use diagnostics, a known-answer model fixture, missing/unseen inputs and sparse-group cases. If there is no demonstrated held-out improvement or support for the input conditions, display `experimental ETA unavailable` or a clearly labeled validated simpler fallback, not a claimed weather benefit.
- **Synthetic limitation:** Existing H-02 fixtures intentionally have no weather-duration relationship and remain unchanged. H-10 supplies separately documented synthetic relationships, known coefficients/noise and a no-weather-effect comparison. Recovery of invented effects validates mechanics, not real EMS accuracy or causal effects. Unobserved starting location, distance, traffic and resource availability limit predictions.
- **QC separation:** I-MR continues to use actual completed response times and fixed neighborhood/priority baselines. ETA prediction errors and prediction-interval exceedances are not control-chart signals. Do not use ETA estimates to revise QC limits or approve a weather-dependent baseline that fails stability review.

## 6. Outputs
- Active-call view showing synthetic incident ID, zone, priority, dispatch status, and elapsed simulated time, updating automatically during replay.
- Supervisor dashboard refreshing as valid arrival events complete incidents.
- CSV and event validation summaries with accepted/rejected counts and reasons.
- Zone and priority filters, baseline dates, monitoring period, and observation counts.
- Individuals and moving-range charts with units, fixed limits, and marked signals.
- Neighborhood boxplots, assignment-hour/weekday median heatmap, and weather-condition boxplots with counts and explanatory labels. Weather context also appears on active incidents and investigation details.
- Incident-level investigation table with the evidence triggering each flag.
- After ETA validation, experimental assignment-time arrival estimate and 95% prediction range, separate from actual response time and QC status. Include wind/gust context and prediction-availability status.
- Descriptive summaries and year-end views covering only the uploaded period.
- Interpretation: `Above the upper control limit means unusually long relative to this group's baseline; investigate circumstances.`
- Distinguish `signal detected`, `no signal detected`, `insufficient baseline data`, `baseline requires review`, `degenerate baseline`, and `baseline unsuitable`. No signal is not proof that service is acceptable.
- No clinical or service target is invented. Control limits describe historical behavior, not acceptable service standards.

## 9. Out of scope for version one
- Real dispatcher-system APIs, real operational dispatch decisions, GPS tracking, GPS-based arrival detection, and parsing private call logs. Simulated active-call updates and synthetic arrival events are in scope.
- Logins, databases, real patient/customer data, operational routing forecasts, causal attribution, and adjusted zone league tables. The explicitly labeled experimental assignment-time ETA is in scope.
- Traffic modeling, resource routing, or claims about fatalities or clinical outcomes. Weather context and the separately validated experimental ETA are in scope; weather-adjusted QC limits, real dispatch ETA claims and live weather APIs remain outside version one.

# SPEC: Emergency Response Quality Monitor

## 1. Team and prompt
- **Team name:** DIIS.
- **Track:** On-campus (24-hour sprint).
- **Prompt we chose:** Health Systems — Emergency Response (team-provided title). The team has not been supplied an official prompt number or a separate exact title; no identifier is invented. Use the team-provided challenge summary below, and reconcile wording if organizers later provide it.
- **Challenge summary (provided by the team):** Ambulance dispatch-to-on-scene times vary across city zones; monthly averages hide extreme delays and patterns requiring investigation.
- **Project repository:** https://github.com/xz1987/six-sigma (current origin; public accessibility must be verified under H-08).

## 2. User and use case
- **Primary user:** The lead EMS worker on the responding ambulance who will arrive at the incident scene, working across five selected Queens neighborhoods. A dispatcher supervisor / analyst is a secondary user of historical analysis.
- **Decision supported:** The responding lead EMS worker monitors their assigned synthetic incident and status. The secondary supervisor reviews two finalized response-system durations: call received to confirmed scene arrival, and transport departure to confirmed hospital arrival. After either duration is complete, the supervisor reviews incidents and neighborhoods requiring investigation. QC flags do not provide routing or clinical instructions.
- **Review workflow:** The Supervisor opens in ordinary mode and reads completed canonical response records from the shared in-app Caller/Responder stream. Confirmed arrival at the destination/scene emits the call-to-scene completion; confirmed arrival at the hospital later emits the transport-to-hospital completion. Each workspace's visible demo action switches the Supervisor to its isolated seeded replay of bundled historical fixtures; selecting Exit demo mode stops and discards the replay and immediately reconnects the dashboard to the ordinary stream. Users do not upload CSV files. All simulated views carry a visible synthetic-data label.
- **Prompt fit:** Incident-level monitoring exposes variation and extreme delays that monthly averages can hide. A flag requests investigation; it does not establish a cause, clinical outcome, or staff fault.

## 3. Inputs and measurement
One historical row represents one incident with timestamps emitted by a response system. Ordinary mode reads an in-memory, process-level canonical stream shared across Caller, Responder, and Supervisor sessions. One incident row is upserted as its milestones complete, while the Supervisor expands it into separate scene-arrival and hospital-arrival completion records. Developer mode alone supplies deterministic replay records. `app/response-metrics.R` defines the shared canonical adapter boundary. The in-memory stream is a connection seam, not durable production storage.

| Column | Type / units | Meaning and source |
|---|---|---|
| `incident_id` | unique text | Synthetic incident identifier; an operational equivalent would come from dispatch logs. |
| `zone` | categorical: `Astoria`, `Flushing`, `Jamaica`, `Forest Hills`, `Far Rockaway` | Selected Queens neighborhood containing the incident; not ambulance home zone. |
| `call_priority` | ordered categories: `High`, `Medium`, `Low` | Synthetic urgency ratings: most urgent, urgent, and lower urgency respectively. Required for stratified analysis; not an official EMS classification. |

Dry means no rain or snow/ice; Snow/Ice takes precedence for mixed winter conditions; Rain otherwise denotes rain; Unknown means unavailable. Synthetic context and, in the separate experimental ETA dataset, a potential predictor; not official weather observations. |

| `scene_arrival_time` | ISO 8601 timestamp with UTC offset | Time the response system confirms the vehicle has arrived at the incident scene; stops the call-to-scene metric. |
| `transport_departure_time` | ISO 8601 timestamp with UTC offset | Time the response system records that the vehicle began the transport leg; starts the transport-to-hospital metric. |

Patterns shows no replay controls. Navigating between workspaces preserves each replay without mixing their records. Restarting or exiting Developer mode restores the documented initial state.

Analyze the two metrics separately; never combine them into one duration or one set of control limits. Scene arrival does not mean patient contact. Hospital arrival means the upstream response system has confirmed both vehicle stop and near-hospital location.
- **Validation:** For each selected metric, report and exclude records with missing incident ID, neighborhood, priority, start or end timestamp; unparseable timestamps; negative duration; duplicate incident ID; or undocumented neighborhood/priority categories. A record may be complete for one metric and pending for the other. Weather is explicitly exempt from exclusion: missing or invalid weather maps to `Unknown`, is reported, and does not exclude an otherwise valid incident from baseline counts, QC, or descriptive analysis. Report counts and reasons; do not impute operational timestamps or remove valid long durations. The deterministic demo adapter is a synthetic fixture mechanism, not production imputation.
- **Cross-zone dispatch:** Assign analysis to the incident zone. Ambulance home zone may be an optional descriptive field; it does not change the grouping definition.
- **Analysis settings:** Explicit baseline start/end and monitoring dates; monitoring begins after the baseline ends. Assign baseline/monitoring membership and chronological order using the selected metric's start timestamp. Event definitions are confirmed for the synthetic demo; any future operational integration must verify the source-system meanings.
- **Weather context:** Use `weather_condition` at assignment time in CSV records and dispatch events; retain it through arrival. Missing weather fields or values map to `Unknown` with a reported count, without excluding otherwise valid incidents from QC. Report invalid nonempty labels as weather validation issues and map them to `Unknown`. Show Unknown as its own descriptive category. Weather is synthetic, supplied by fixtures rather than a live weather API.

## 4. Statistics and validation
- **Core method:** Individuals and moving-range (I-MR) charts for each finalized duration separately—call received to scene arrival, and transport departure to hospital arrival—then separately by incident zone and call priority in chronological order. Estimate metric-specific fixed limits from an initial 12-month baseline and apply them to subsequent monitoring observations.
- **Why it fits:** Each completed metric pair supplies one continuous duration. The method monitors unusual durations and changes between consecutive incidents within a comparable metric group.
- **Reference:** [NIST individuals control charts](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc322.htm).
- **Individuals calculation:** Baseline mean `xbar`; consecutive absolute moving ranges with mean `MRbar`; `sigma_hat = MRbar / 1.128`; individuals limits `xbar +/- 3 * sigma_hat`. Retain mathematical limits; explain that negative durations are invalid even if the calculated lower limit is negative.
- **Completed metric records only:** A selected duration reaches QC only after both of its required response-system timestamps exist. Incomplete durations remain pending and are not I-MR observations. New response-system records never change a frozen baseline.
- **Primary investigation rule:** A monitoring duration strictly above its selected metric/zone/priority group's fixed individuals upper control limit flags that incident and its zone for investigation. Show the metric, start time, value, limit, priority, and baseline dates.
- **Variation chart:** Moving-range center `MRbar`, lower limit zero, upper limit `3.267 * MRbar`. Label upper-limit exceedances as variation signals, distinct from unusually long durations. Calculate ranges only within the same metric/zone/priority sequence, ordered by that metric's start timestamp with incident ID as a deterministic tie-breaker. The first completed monitoring observation uses the last baseline observation in that same group as its predecessor; later completed observations use the preceding observation in start-time order. Never form ranges across metrics or groups. The boundary range is a monitoring value and is excluded from baseline MRbar and limit estimation. When records complete out of start-time order, recompute the affected monitoring sequence from available completed records; pending metric pairs have no completed-duration observation.
- **Supporting descriptive summaries:** Valid metric-record count, median, and 90th-percentile duration in minutes, using R's `quantile(..., type = 7)`. These require their own known-answer checks before display.
- **Additional descriptive charts:** Neighborhood duration boxplots for the selected metric, priority and date range; hour-of-day by weekday heatmap of median duration for a selected metric/neighborhood/priority; weather-condition boxplots for a selected metric/neighborhood/priority and date range. Include sample counts and units on every chart. These charts use completed valid metric records and do not create additional investigation rules.
- **Chart definitions:** Derive selected-metric start hour (0-23) and weekday in `America/New_York`, accounting for daylight saving time. Heatmap cells show median and count; empty cells show no data, never zero duration. Boxplots use type-7 quartiles, whiskers to the most extreme observations within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the quartiles, and separate markers beyond the whiskers. Markers are descriptive, not I-MR signals or grounds for deletion. Sparse groups display individual points and counts; no precision or adjusted-ranking claim is made.
- **Chart validation:** Under H-03, independently verify grouping, medians, counts, quartiles, whiskers, weather-missing handling, and local-time conversion before rendering under H-04/H-05. Include an empty heatmap cell and a sparse weather category.
- **Zone and priority comparison:** Retain raw descriptive charts with sample counts. Add a separate marginal comparison from a frozen synthetic log-duration model using zone, priority, assignment-time weather, hour band and weekday. Report adjusted geometric means with 95% model intervals and reference counts. Label these as descriptive associations, not causal effects or operational league tables. Staffing and traffic remain unmeasured; traffic is explicitly unavailable in the canonical stream.
- **Assumptions:** Assess baseline stability, distribution shape, and serial dependence within each group. Use time plots, distribution diagnostics, and lag diagnostics. Strong skew or dependence can invalidate conventional limit interpretation; revisit the method before presenting validated signals if diagnostics are unsuitable. A 12-month baseline spans a full annual cycle but does not automatically adjust for seasonality. Review monthly patterns; unresolved seasonal structure requires revisiting the method before interpreting fixed limits.
- **Baseline eligibility:** Select 12 consecutive calendar months preceding monitoring. Each zone/priority group needs at least 100 valid incidents, with observations in every baseline month. Report monthly counts. The count and coverage thresholds are project safeguards, not proof of stability or a full seasonal adjustment. Groups failing either threshold show `insufficient baseline data`; descriptive summaries remain available, but monitoring flags are disabled.
- **Threshold reference:** [Minitab control-chart guidance](https://support.minitab.com/minitab/media/pdfs/translate/Assistant_Variables_Control_Charts.pdf) supports 100 observations for control-limit precision. Requiring coverage in all 12 months is a team-chosen safeguard.
- **Baseline review:** One teammate prepares chronological plots, preliminary I-MR charts, distribution diagnostics, and lag/autocorrelation diagnostics; another reviews them. Record group, baseline dates, reviewer, decision, and rationale. Review unusual points, shifts, trends, strong skew, serial dependence, and changes in dispatch procedures, priority definitions, or recording practices. See [NIST process-stability guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section4/ppc45.htm).
- **Approval and exceptions:** Before documented approval, or while concerns remain unresolved, show `baseline requires review` and disable monitoring flags. Review may approve the baseline with documented justification or mark it `baseline unsuitable` pending a revised period or method. Do not use a single normality test as automatic approval. Investigate valid extreme observations rather than automatically deleting them; correct documented errors and record any justified exclusions or reference-period changes. Do not repeatedly remove points merely to obtain a passing chart.
- **Degenerate baseline:** A zero average moving range produces `degenerate baseline`; monitoring flags are disabled. Approval cannot override insufficient data or zero variation.
- **Fixed reference:** Once eligible and approved, freeze baseline observations and limits separately for each metric/zone/priority group during monitoring. Switching metrics selects a different fixed reference; it never refits limits from the visible data. Any later rebaseline requires an explicit, documented review; monitoring observations do not silently update limits.
- **Supplementary signal rules:** In addition to the primary Individuals and moving-range rules, the Supervisor may label the monitoring endpoint of six common SPC patterns: one point beyond 3σ, two of three beyond 2σ on the same side, four of five beyond 1σ on the same side, eight consecutive points on one side, six-point monotonic trend, and fifteen consecutive points within 1σ. These labels use the already-frozen center and sigma estimate and never refit limits. Group same-day signals by metric, zone, priority and primary rule in the inbox; preserve incident-level evidence in the investigation drawer.
- **Daily rational subgroups:** For the selected metric, zone and priority, aggregate observations by completed New York calendar day. Always show the full 2025 baseline, then append canonical monitoring days only after they have ended; calls from the currently open day remain live I-MR observations and do not become a subgroup until midnight. The subgroup view is independent of the day/week/month incident-display window. Show daily mean and `n`, where `n` is the count of completed response records in that calendar-day subgroup, with variable 3σ limits `xbar +/- 3*sigma_hat/sqrt(n)`, bounded at zero on the lower side. This is a supplemental view of daily means; it does not replace the incident-level I-MR chart.
- **Contextual response goal:** Ordinary mode displays a frozen synthetic reference-model goal analogous to the ETA workflow. The log-duration model uses the 2025 reference records and the available zone, priority, assignment-time weather, hour band and weekday fields. Define the demo goal as the model's conditional 90th percentile, `exp(fitted log duration + qnorm(.90) * residual sigma)`, so a median prediction is not incorrectly treated as an exceedance threshold. A completed record above that goal receives a `Contextual target miss` performance label; this does not alter I-MR limits. When a live view is empty, the displayed zone/priority goal is standardized over the reference weather/time mix. Traffic is not in the canonical contract and must display as unavailable. Developer mode replaces this goal with an explicitly manual demo-only value for each metric.
- **Data-quality separation:** Missing identifiers, duplicate identifiers, invalid or reversed timestamps, and unavailable weather context appear in a distinct data-quality panel. They do not count as performance signals. Developer mode may trigger a labeled demonstration warning without inserting a fake operational record.
- **Known-answer check:** For the arithmetic fixture `4, 5, 6, 5, 4` minutes: mean `4.8`, moving ranges `1, 1, 1, 1`, `MRbar = 1`, `sigma_hat = 1 / 1.128`, individuals UCL approximately `7.459574`, LCL approximately `2.140426`, and MR UCL `3.267`. A subsequent value `8` exceeds the individuals UCL. The baseline-to-monitoring range is `abs(8 - 4) = 4`, which also exceeds the MR UCL `3.267`; neither baseline estimate changes. This five-point fixture tests arithmetic only, not baseline adequacy.
- **Degenerate checks:** Constant baseline, one observation, missing or invalid timestamps, empty groups, duplicate IDs, tied timestamps, and unequal group sizes. A zero moving range must produce an explicit degenerate-baseline status rather than divide-by-zero behavior or a validated all-clear.
- **Implementation:** Statistical calculations live in plain, framework-independent functions in `app/qc.R`; the UI calls them. Use `testthat` for known-answer and edge-case checks. Verify each reported statistic independently before styling.
- **Repository dependency:** Statistical implementation follows `.claude/skills/stats-first-steering/SKILL.md`, restored by the repository merge.

### Experimental weather-informed arrival estimate (H-10 through H-12)
- **Purpose:** Give the responding lead EMS worker an estimated assignment-to-on-scene duration and arrival-time range at assignment, labeled `Experimental — synthetic data`. This is a broad incident-duration prediction, not GPS routing, patient-contact prediction, or validated operational guidance.
- **Candidate model:** Start with an interpretable R regression of log positive response minutes on neighborhood, priority, assignment-time hour/weekday, weather type, precipitation intensity, and numerical sustained wind/gust features. Specify the final formula, category encoding, collinearity handling and prediction method in H-11 before implementation. Severity categories do not translate directly into minutes. Keep ETA functions in `app/eta.R`, separate from `app/qc.R`.
- **Timing:** Use only inputs available at assignment, never actual arrival or eventual response duration. Add predicted duration to assignment time to obtain the arrival estimate. Predictions are fixed at assignment; do not show a continually recalculated remaining-time estimate without a separately validated conditional model. If simulated time passes the predicted window, show that it has elapsed rather than a negative countdown.
- **Uncertainty:** Show a 95% prediction interval for an individual future duration and the corresponding arrival timestamps. If using log-scale regression, exponentiating the fitted log response gives a conditional median, not the arithmetic mean; label it appropriately. Validate prediction intervals and their assumptions, not only coefficient estimates. See [NIST prediction intervals](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section5/pmd512.htm).
- **Validation:** Use chronological training/validation/test partitions and report their dates. Freeze model choices before the untouched final test; keep shared weather-event blocks together to prevent leakage. Compare with an otherwise comparable model without weather, reporting held-out MAE in minutes, 95% interval coverage and widths, sample counts, and performance by priority/weather category. Set quantitative acceptance gates before fitting in H-11. Use diagnostics, a known-answer model fixture, missing/unseen inputs and sparse-group cases. If there is no demonstrated held-out improvement or support for the input conditions, display `experimental ETA unavailable` or a clearly labeled validated simpler fallback, not a claimed weather benefit.
- **Synthetic limitation:** Existing H-02 fixtures intentionally have no weather-duration relationship and remain unchanged. H-10 supplies separately documented synthetic relationships, known coefficients/noise and a no-weather-effect comparison. Recovery of invented effects validates mechanics, not real EMS accuracy or causal effects. Unobserved starting location, distance, traffic and resource availability limit predictions.
- **QC separation:** I-MR uses the two actual completed response-system durations separately with fixed metric/neighborhood/priority baselines. ETA prediction errors and prediction-interval exceedances are not control-chart signals. Do not use ETA estimates to revise QC limits or approve a weather-dependent baseline that fails stability review.

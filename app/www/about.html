DIIS · Emergency Response Monitor
About this demonstration

Find the delays averages can miss.

The DIIS Emergency Response Monitor is a hackathon prototype exploring how incident-level response-time data can help supervisors spot unusual delays and see where follow-up may be needed.

Synthetic demonstration only. This app does not contact 911 or EMS, dispatch an ambulance, provide clinical advice, or provide operational navigation. For an actual emergency in the United States, call 911.

Choose a role

Supervisor

Review shared response records, control charts, summaries, and investigation signals for scene and hospital arrival times.

Primary responder

Accept a synthetic incident, follow its demo progress, review scenario-matched hospital options, and confirm arrival.

Caller

Create a synthetic tracking link and follow the same incident shared with the responder and supervisor. No call or message is sent.

Staff sign-ins use disposable credentials displayed in the login dialog. Caller access does not require a staff account.

What the analysis shows

The supervisor monitors two durations separately: call received to confirmed scene arrival, and transport departure to confirmed hospital arrival. Individuals and Moving Range charts organize completed synthetic records by zone and simulated call priority. A signal asks for investigation; it does not explain the cause or assign fault.

Read the Supervisor views

Active tracking follows completed incidents; Patterns compares descriptive distributions; History analysis reviews completed periods with independent filters and a replayable tutorial. After the charts, What this view tells you explains the selected results, sample counts and interpretation limits. Expand How to read this chart for chart guidance.

Try demo mode reveals a normal or delayed 12-case day. Its generated records stay separate from the connected Caller/Responder trip. Historical signals, contextual targets and editable service goals are different comparisons; none establishes a cause.

Current development

  • Caller, responder, and supervisor views share one in-memory synthetic incident.
  • Five selected Queens zones, documented hospital services, and stored road-route examples support the demonstration.
  • Responder weather and traffic prompts are authored examples, not live conditions or measured time savings.
  • Data, locations, routes, conditions, and replay times are synthetic or bundled snapshots. Hospital availability is not checked.

The app has no live dispatch, GPS, traffic, weather, or hospital-capacity feed, and no durable incident database. Demo state clears when the server process restarts. Real system integration, secure access and audit controls, and operational validation remain future work.
